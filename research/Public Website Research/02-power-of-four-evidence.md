# Center for Bible Engagement: evidence and copy guidance

Primary sources checked October 24, 2026. This is a research attribution, not a partnership or endorsement.

## Sources and findings

**Cole and Ovwigho, December 2009:** the report describes online surveys involving over 40,000 Americans, mixing random and non-random samples. The reported behavioral analysis includes a selected cohort of 8,665 Christian adults. Those figures describe different populations and must not be interchanged. See methods and behavioral findings on printed pages 3–7 of [Scientific Evidence for the Power of 4](https://bttbfiles.com/web/docs/cbe/Scientific_Evidence_for_the_Power_of_4.pdf).

**Cole and Ovwigho, August 2012:** the synthesis covers 108,196 survey participants across multiple studies. It associates engagement on at least four days per week with several reported measures of spiritual growth. The study definition includes encountering Scripture, considering its meaning and responding. See printed pages 2–3 and 5–6 of [Bible Engagement as the Key to Spiritual Growth](https://bttbfiles.com/web/docs/cbe/Research_Synthesis_Bible_Engagement_and_Spiritual_Growth_Aug2012.pdf). The [current ministry research listing](https://int.odbm.org/en/center-for-bible-engagement/research-findings/synthesis-bible-engagement-as-the-key-to-spiritual-growth) provides another discovery route to that work.

These are survey-based relationships. Treating the reported associations as causal proof, a universal four-day threshold, or evidence that Rooted itself changes outcomes would go beyond what this design can establish.

## Recommended treatment on the page

The section is named **“Four days. A deeper rhythm.”** It pairs a concise attribution with four illustrated weekday moments. The graphic is an illustrative practice rhythm, not a statistical chart or a measured Rooted result. The actual research is one click away.

Do not lead with viral percentages. A headline percentage would need its precise outcome, comparison, population and interpretation preserved; odds are not interchangeable with percentage-point changes or individual risk. The qualitative treatment is clearer for this introduction and avoids presenting research about Scripture as a promise about software.

The design does not count church attendance or app visits as Bible engagement. Its example provides four weekday invitations to interact with Scripture independently of Sunday attendance. Reading days and schedules remain choices for the church, rather than an inflexible membership requirement.

## Attribution and assets

Use CBE’s name as a source credit and link to the report. Do not place a CBE logo among customer or partner badges. Do not imply that the organization reviewed Rooted. The proposed public footnote explicitly separates the research from Rooted’s own claims.

The four-day graphic is original artwork; it does not reproduce CBE’s report figures. No third-party research PDF or infographic is republished in this package. Recheck destination links and intended attribution with the final copy before public launch.

## Claims to leave out

- “Use Rooted four times and your life will change.”
- “Four app opens equal four Scripture engagements.”
- “Missing a fourth day means your Bible reading had no value.”
- “CBE proves Rooted improves family relationships.”
- Unqualified claims of clinical, causal or guaranteed outcomes.

The role of this evidence is to explain why a consistent rhythm matters. The product’s role is to help the church make room for that practice.

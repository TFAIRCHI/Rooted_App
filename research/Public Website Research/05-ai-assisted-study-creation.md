# AI-assisted study creation: benefits and public-page content

Added October 24, 2026 to make the Study Co-writer a visible part of Rooted’s public introduction. The homepage should explain how the church gets from its teaching to usable studies, rather than treating AI as a small drafting reference in the process diagram.

## The promise to communicate

**A little input. A helpful head start.**

Start with an idea, a Scripture passage, or detailed sermon notes. Rooted’s AI co-writer asks questions, shapes a plan with you, and helps create complete daily devotionals for your team to review.

The value is practical: a church can develop its own teaching into a manageable week of studies without writing every outline, reflection and question from a blank page. The pastor’s message and the team’s decisions remain the direction for the work.

## Two ways to begin

| Starting point | Example | How the co-writer helps |
|---|---|---|
| A simple request | “Help me plan three adult devotionals for next week about trusting God.” | Clarifies the passage, audience, reading length, dates and intended takeaway before proposing the studies |
| More extensive material | A sermon summary, outline, approved passage references, illustrations and detailed teaching notes | Helps organize the supplied material, preserve the central message, and develop a useful progression across the week |

A simple request can begin the conversation immediately. It does not mean a vague prompt can silently become a published, source-verified study. The co-writer asks for the teaching basis and missing details as the plan develops.

Detailed input should be described as supported notes and text, not unlimited input. A `.txt` attachment or a large source field does not remove the current processing limits. Full-length transcript processing, automatic YouTube ingestion, and audio/video examination must remain future capabilities until connected and verified. This marketing update does not implement those features or change the application’s input limits.

## Benefits to the church

- **Less starting from scratch.** Get help turning the church’s notes into an outline, daily explanations, reflection prompts and practical applications. This is an intended reduction in preparation burden, not a measured time-saving percentage.
- **A consistent teaching thread.** Carry the sermon’s central idea through the week instead of preparing unrelated daily topics. Use follow-up questions to keep the drafts faithful to the intended direction.
- **A better fit for the people reading.** Discuss audience, reading length, familiarity with Scripture, tone, illustrations and family use. Ask for a simpler explanation or a deeper treatment where appropriate.
- **A clear progression over several days.** Agree on the overall direction, then develop and confirm each day. A later day can build on the previous one while still having its own focus and application.
- **Complete studies in the workspace.** Confirmed creation fills the ordinary saved editor fields: title, references, body, reflection, discussion question, application and message preview. A closing prayer can be requested in the devotional body. The result is an editable draft, rather than text that must be manually copied out of chat.
- **More time for ministry judgment.** Give the team a concrete draft to discuss and refine. People can focus their attention on faithful teaching, the needs of the congregation and meaningful next steps.

These are product capabilities and proposed benefits. Do not invent claims such as “cuts preparation by 90%,” “replaces a staff role,” or “guarantees accurate theology.”

## The process to show

**Bring an idea or notes → discuss the direction → review the plan → confirm each day → review the saved drafts → publish when ready.**

The homepage visual should demonstrate a conversation, a focused clarification, and a short proposed week. Label the visual as an example. The brief plan is the result of discussion, not a claim that one sentence instantly completed the work. The main process diagram should explicitly call the assistant an **AI co-writer**.

## A small safeguards section

Use the heading **“Helpful AI. Your church stays in charge.”** Keep this short on the public page:

1. **Agree before creating.** Review the focus, audience, notes and dates before studies are created.
2. **Confirm each day.** Each day waits for its own confirmation before becoming a saved draft.
3. **Review before publishing.** People check Scripture, teaching and wording. The co-writer does not independently publish to members.
4. **Keep the church context private.** Conversations are scoped to the signed-in contributor and church; one church’s discussion is not another church’s context.

Do not turn this into a promise of infallible output, universal doctrinal approval, zero provider retention or a particular security certification. The public explanation should emphasize control and responsible review without overwhelming the benefits section.

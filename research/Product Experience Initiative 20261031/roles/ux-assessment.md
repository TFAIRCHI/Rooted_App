# UX assessment and rendered concept

October 31, 2026. This is a heuristic UX evaluation informed by source inspection and simulated contributor, engineering, and member roles. It is not research with actual church staff or members. All prototype files and images are confined to this initiative; no application code or production service was changed.

## Recommended experience

Keep one selected study and its church, date/timezone, saved revision, and unresolved review status in view across six clear tasks: **Overview, Sources, CoWriter, Studio Creator, Share deck, Review**. A compact desktop action rail keeps the current task's next step available while the document scrolls normally. At smaller widths it becomes ordinary document content. Do not put an independently scrolling phone reader inside an independently scrolling editor.

CoWriter is a verbal collaborator for teaching, planning, and draft proposals. Studio Creator is a separate visual workspace with ordered blocks, selected content, and an inspector. Both operate on the same revisioned study rather than maintaining two copies of the teaching. A full-page immersive preview should use the same authored content and rendering contract as the actual member reader.

The research storyboard demonstrates a shared fictional six-block renderer and reorderable sample model. This does **not** implement the application's persisted block model, migration, storage, media processing, member renderer, or approval contract. The current plain-text reader cannot gain visual support merely by adding Studio tabs.

## Role critique and corrections

The contributor role identified a deck/source-revision inconsistency, inaccurate reviewed-source count, and excessive desktop chrome. These were corrected: the current draft stays blocked from approval, a separately labeled approved-source case demonstrates the review action, one included/one pending source is counted honestly, and source/Studio/review actions moved to a desktop rail. The root agent independently identified source-confirm and Studio-preview controls below the first viewport; the final browser checks measure those controls at 1366×768 and 1440×900.

The member role identified a wrong-church transition, missing public-copy disclosure, and a share cancellation path that did not return to reading. The corrected prototype preserves the selected church through verification, reload, enrollment, reading, sharing, and cancellation. It makes reading the primary action and returns cancellation to the reflection anchor. Anyone may forward shared/downloaded cards; copies cannot be recalled. Sharing and study reminders start off.

The engineering role identified that list-only reordering did not establish preview parity. The corrected prototype uses one `teachingBlocks` model and `readingBlocks()` renderer, with the same ordered IDs in immersive and member rendition previews. Both explicitly remain unpublished research previews; production members must see only an approved published snapshot. Independent engineering checks verified the actual DOM block order, rather than accepting descriptive copy as proof.

## Acceptance criteria for the eventual implementation

1. At desktop 1366×768 and 1440×900, source confirmation with consent, Studio order/preview actions, and review reply/resubmit entry are visible without scrolling; saved revision and review context are visible. Long content can still use one natural document scroll.
2. At 320px width, task controls, source review, reading, and sharing reflow with no horizontal page overflow or hover-only requirements. Restore focus after transitions; preserve selected church and reading position.
3. Reload and reopen must reproduce saved blocks/media/order. The preview, published payload, and member renderer must agree on exact authored content, asset revision, and sequence. Test zero fallback teaching insertion.
4. A new source or URL is never sent to AI before selected-excerpt review and confirmation. Failed extraction reports what did not happen and offers a valid recovery. Excel selected cells carry sheet/range provenance and formula-value status.
5. Adoption creates a new draft revision with an explicit replacement preview. Concurrent changes require comparison/recovery. The original saved study remains until adoption; approval/publication are separate.
6. Deck approval binds source study revision, image/rendition version, caption, public destination, and church/Rooted branding. Future export/share pauses when those change. Actual private study/member data never enter the public rendition.
7. Request-changes submission note and reason roundtrip from real storage/API to visible UI. A reply persists with its anchor. A same-revision retry recovers clearly; a newly saved revision targets a new review cycle without losing history.
8. Verified identity never implicitly grants workspace permissions, merges household accounts, or establishes formal church membership. QR enrollment follows explicit church policy and protects the right tenant. Recovery and alternative sign-in work when SMS is unavailable.
9. The homepage recording depicts the actually connected workflow and has real captions/transcript. A poster/play icon must not pretend that a recording or model response exists.

## Evidence and limitations

See [mockup guide](../mockups/README.md), [capture manifest](../mockups/captures-manifest.json), and [browser validation](../mockups/browser-validation.json). All 37 task checks pass, with no horizontal page overflow across 35 reflow layouts, no script errors, and no external browser requests. A separate [57-control keyboard focus audit](../mockups/keyboard-focus-validation.json) passes after correcting action-rail focus obscuration through document scroll padding/control margins. Independent role notes and engineering evidence are separate initiative artifacts. Checks exercise local visual interactions, keyboard controls, reflow, revision messaging, sharing defaults, and sample block order. They do not prove production auth, provider support, ingestion safety, delivery, durable media, approval authorization, or publication.
